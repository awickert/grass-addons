DESCRIPTION

r.flexure computes how the rigid outer shell of a planet deforms elastically in response to surface-normal loads by solving equations for plate bending. This phenomenon is known as "flexural isostasy" and is relevant to glacier/ice-cap/ice-sheet loading, sedimentary basin filling, mountain belt growth, volcano emplacement, sea-level change, and other geologic processes. r.flexure and v.flexure are the GRASS GIS interfaces to the model gFlex. As both r.flexure and v.flexure are interfaces to gFlex, it must be downloaded and installed. The most recent versions of gFlex are available from https://github.com/awickert/gFlex, and installation instructions are available on that page via the README.md file.

NOTES

The parameter method selects the solution technique:
FD — Finite Difference
Typically faster for large grids and supports spatially variable elastic thickness via the van Wees and Cloetingh (1994) stencil. Memory requirements scale with grid size; choose a grid spacing appropriate for the flexural wavelength of the problem. Supports all boundary conditions and in-plane stresses.
FFT — Fast Fourier Transform (spectral)
Exact for periodic boundary conditions; uses a zero-padded approximation for all other boundary conditions (equivalent to NoOutsideLoads). Requires a scalar (uniform) elastic thickness. Supports in-plane stresses. Fastest option for large uniform-Te grids.
SAS — Superposition of Analytical Solutions
Each grid cell is treated as a point load and its deflection computed analytically. Best for smaller grids or when accuracy near individual loads matters. Requires scalar elastic thickness.

The flexural solution is generated for the current computational region, so be sure to check g.region before running the module.

input is a 2-D raster of loads in units of stress [Pa], equal to the load material density times gravitational acceleration times column thickness. This is computed by the user prior to running the module, and is not affected by the g parameter below (which applies only to the restoring isostatic force).

te, written in standard notation as Te, is the lithospheric elastic thickness. It may be either a scalar value or (for FD only) a raster of spatially variable elastic thickness.

Boundary conditions govern the behavior of the plate at the domain edges. Their meaning depends on the solution method:

For SAS, all boundary conditions default to NoOutsideLoads, which assumes the plate extends to infinity beyond the domain and that no loads act there.

For FFT, set all boundary conditions to Periodic for an exact periodic transform (loads wrap around from one edge to the opposite). Any other value selects the zero-padded (NoOutsideLoads) approximation.

For FD, the available boundary conditions are:

0Displacement0Slope
Zero displacement and zero slope at the boundary. Use for a plate clamped to a rigid wall, or when the far-field deflection is known to be zero. Note: if the nearest loaded cell is within one flexural wavelength of this boundary, the forebulge may be suppressed; gFlex will issue a warning.
0Moment0Shear
"Free broken end" (e.g., rifted margin): the second and third derivatives of displacement are zero. Equivalent to the end of a free-hanging diving board.
0Slope0Shear
First and third derivatives of displacement are zero. Used to approximate a symmetric boundary.
Mirror
Loads and elastic thickness are reflected at the boundary.
Periodic
Wrap-around: the eastern and western edges (or northern and southern) are treated as adjacent. Must be applied in matched pairs (both East–West or both North–South).

Boundary conditions may be mixed freely, except that Periodic must be applied symmetrically. To minimize boundary effects, place domain edges at least one flexural wavelength from the nearest load.

In-plane stresses (sigma_xx, sigma_yy, sigma_xy) add membrane (tectonic) stress terms to the governing equation, following the full variable-D formulation. These are supported by the FD and FFT solvers and default to 0 (no in-plane stress). Positive values are tensional.

The -p flag enables automatic domain padding for variable-Te FD runs. When elastic thickness varies spatially, abrupt rigidity gradients at domain edges can produce spurious deflections via the D-derivative terms in the FD stencil. The padding flag calls gflex.pad_domain() to extend the domain by one flexural wavelength on each side (with a smoothly tapered Te transition), solves on the padded domain, then trims the output back to the original GRASS region. Ignored for scalar Te or non-FD methods.

r.flexure may be run in latitude/longitude coordinates with the -l flag. Because the module requires a single dx and dy, it computes dx at the midpoint latitude of the domain. This approximation degrades near the poles.
